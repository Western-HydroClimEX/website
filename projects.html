Case study

Mapping a compound storm in Tofino

Tofino sits on a narrow peninsula on Vancouver Island, exposed to Pacific storms on one side and inlet flooding on the other. We rebuilt three days of a late November 2021 storm in a two-dimensional flood model to see where water went, when it arrived and how long it stayed.

Preliminary simulations show flooding concentrated along low-lying shorelines and inlet margins. Model evaluation against observed water levels, sea-level rise scenarios and a machine learning emulator trained on these runs are ongoing.

Saint Lucia, Caribbean

Compound flooding and cascading infrastructure failure

Saint Lucia has short rain records and steep catchments that drain straight to the sea. We built a probabilistic flood model combining rainfall, river flow and waves for Tropical Storm Matthew (2016), then traced how flooding at the international airport spreads through operations, roads and tourism, and how fast each recovers.

Years
2018 to 2025

For Environment and Climate Change Canada we analysed the climate variables behind extreme maximum flows in the Lake Winnipeg watershed. The question now runs basin-wide in the Great Lakes, classifying simulated floods by mechanism: rain, snowmelt, rain on snow or wet soils.
