Research

Research themes

Four connected programs, from storm and climate processes to the values engineers design for. Each program links to the projects, people and papers behind it.

Program 1

Compound flood processes

When rain, river flow, storm surge and waves peak together, the flood is worse than any one of them suggests. We study compound flooding on Canada's Pacific and Atlantic coasts, in the Great Lakes and in inland basins, separate it by storm type, and project how warming changes it. Work on Arctic coasts is in development with federal partners.

  • Copulas and nonstationary joint return levels
  • Storm typing: extratropical cyclones, atmospheric rivers, post-tropical storms
  • Physical coastal processes: waves, setup, runup, overtopping and erosion (a developing research line, started 2026)
Key papersAtmospheric rivers as triggers of compound flooding: quantifying extreme joint events in western North America under climate change (Natural Hazards and Earth System Sciences, 2026)Multivariate analysis of compound flood hazard across Canada's Atlantic, Pacific and Great Lakes coastal areas (Earth's Future, 2022)Nonstationary frequency analysis of compound flooding in Canada's coastal zones (Coastal Engineering, 2023)

People: Jiajin Feng, Behnaz Hadi, Yasaman Taleghani, Mehrdad Jafarzadeh, Tianze Pang

Program 2

Hydroclimatic swings and changing extremes

How extremes are changing and why. A core line of work is hydroclimatic swings, also called weather whiplash: rapid transitions from drought to deluge, or wet to dry, that strain reservoirs, dams and drainage. Using large climate model ensembles, we separate human influence from natural variability and project how swings, successive and lagged flood and drought events, rain on snow and short-duration design rainfall change with warming.

  • Dry to wet and wet to dry swings across North America, the Great Lakes and East Asia
  • Successive and lagged compound floods and droughts
  • Climate-adjusted IDF curves and probable maximum precipitation
  • Detection and attribution with large ensembles

Program 3

Flood modelling, remote sensing and risk

We translate flood simulations into estimates of exposure, infrastructure disruption and risk. We build hydrologic and hydrodynamic model chains, train fast emulators on them, map floods from satellite radar, and use deep learning on street-level imagery and LiDAR to describe the buildings in harm's way.

  • WRF-Hydro, Raven, SFINCS, LISFLOOD-FP, TELEMAC-2D and PCSWMM
  • Machine learning emulators and regional flood frequency
  • Urban flood mapping from Sentinel-1 and RADARSAT Constellation Mission radar with deep learning
  • Building exposure from Google Street View, LiDAR and orthophotos: first floor heights and land use
  • Satellite and radar precipitation (IMERG, MRMS) evaluated and bias-adjusted across Canada
Key papersUrban flood mapping using Sentinel-1 and RADARSAT Constellation Mission image and convolutional Siamese network (Natural Hazards, 2024)Automated first floor height estimation for flood vulnerability analysis using deep learning and Google Street View (Journal of Flood Risk Management, 2024)Fusion of Google Street View, LiDAR and orthophoto classifications for building land-use type detection (Remote Sensing, 2024)Machine learning-based regional flood frequency framework for climate resilient infrastructure (Journal of Hydrology, 2025)A comprehensive evaluation of GPM-IMERG V06 and MRMS with hourly ground-based precipitation observations across Canada (Journal of Hydrology, 2021)Characterizing compound inland flooding mechanisms and risks in North America under climate change (Earth's Future, 2025)

People: Mehrdad Jafarzadeh, Omar Abusanina, Jiajin Feng; alumni Nafiseh Ghasemian Sorboni, Md. Robiul Islam, Saber Moazami, Mohammad Fereshtehpour

Program 4

Infrastructure and multihazard resilience

Floods, hail and wind knock out power, water and transport, and the failures cascade. We trace those chains with network risk methods and dynamic Bayesian networks, first in Saint Lucia, and study the storms behind them, including hail in Alberta through the Northern Hail Project. Much of this work runs through Western's Centre for Multihazard Risk and Resilience, which Reza Najafi cofounded and co-directs.

  • Cascading failures and recovery in interconnected infrastructure
  • Multi-state and fuzzy Bayesian networks for expert-informed risk
  • Compound hail, wind and rain extremes
  • Wind-driven rain and hail on buildings
Key papersIntegrated Bayesian network and strongest path method (BN-SPM) for effective multi-hazard risk assessment of interconnected infrastructure systems (Sustainable Cities and Society, 2024)A flood risk assessment framework for interdependent infrastructure systems in coastal environments (Sustainable Cities and Society, 2021)Multivariate analysis of compound hail, wind and rainfall extremes in Alberta's hail alley (Weather and Climate Extremes, 2024)A dynamic Bayesian network approach to characterize multi-hazard risks and resilience in interconnected critical infrastructures (Reliability Engineering and System Safety, 2025)Influence of large-scale teleconnection patterns on hailstorm severity in Alberta (Geophysical Research Letters, 2025)

People: Issam Mohamed, Yasaman Masaeli; alumni Soheil Bakhtiari and Ying (Katrina) Zhang; with CMRR colleagues
